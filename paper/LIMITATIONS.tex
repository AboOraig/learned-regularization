\section*{Limitations}
% =====================================================

\paragraph{Scope.} The digit experiments are intentionally small ($28\times28$) so that a thorough grid is possible; trends need not transfer to clinical resolution, and the CT study adds only one architecture (a DnCNN trained for one run on 8\,960 of the 35\,820 training slices), so it does not separate the prior from this network. Only two training distributions were compared, so the explanation of the asymmetric transfer between them is a hypothesis. The PnP scheme has no convergence guarantee, we did not measure the Lipschitz constant of the denoiser, and only moderate forward-model mismatches (angles up to $4^\circ$, one jitter draw per level) were evaluated, with hyperparameters frozen at their nominal values.

\paragraph{Baselines and tuning.} PnP has two tuned hyperparameters and TV one. The box-constrained TV control (E2b) was run for E1--E2, E4, E6 (bicubic variant only), E8 (the MNIST- and Fashion-trained families, not the second MNIST family) and E9; the TV baseline of E3, E5, E7, E10 and of the Poisson study (E12--E13) is the unconstrained TV-ADMM, so the corresponding gaps may be overstated; in the Gaussian MNIST case the box constraint lowered the gap by about $0.5$\,dB (E8). On CT, TV is tuned on a grid with steps of a factor three, TV-long is sometimes worse than TV, PSNR was the tuning criterion for every method, which favours accuracy over detectability, and two optima of PnP-TVinit lie on the boundary of the grid. The PnP variants of E15 were introduced after the first PnP-const result.

\paragraph{Data and simulation.} E3, E5 and the original E4 use data generated with the reconstruction operator (an inverse crime); E3 and E5 were not repeated on finer-grid data, and the sensitivity band of Table~\ref{tab:crime} shows that the near noise-free ranking depends on this choice, which we adopted as a convention after seeing all variants. The Poisson study uses a single attenuation scale ($\mu=2$), one angle count and one MNIST-trained denoiser in the dose sweep, and its Shepp--Logan result is one image with one noise draw per dose. The matrix-free operator was validated against the dense operator only at $28\times28$ (agreement limited by single-precision arithmetic, timings from single runs). The CT simulator was checked against real data on 8 slices and one noise draw per condition, and only for the TV baseline; the real data have more variance than the simulation at high counts, and we did not verify that the PnP--TV difference agrees between real and simulated data. LoDoPaB's ground truth is itself a reconstruction.

\paragraph{Statistics.} Seed variation comes from three denoisers per digit configuration, which gives only a crude standard deviation; the CT study has 40 test patients (one slice each) and one noise realisation per condition, so a single cell has an AUC uncertainty of about $\pm0.05$, and the conditions share the same patients and are not independent. No correction for multiplicity was applied. The analysis was developed in steps after seeing results (Section~\ref{sec:history3}): the false-blob definition, the threshold sweep and the shift extension to $K=30,120$ were not pre-specified, and the Poisson lesion experiment was run at only two of the five doses ($I_0=100$ and $30$).

\paragraph{Task-based evaluation.} The lesions are bright additive Gaussian blobs (on CT of 120--240\,HU under an assumed mapping from $\mu/\mu_{\max}$ to HU), the observer is a location-known local-contrast statistic, false blobs are defined by this statistic and a threshold, and no reader study was done, so false-lesion rates are meaningful for comparing methods but not as absolute rates. The observability analysis is confounded with lesion size and location. The operating-point study (E10) uses one denoiser per prior, two lesion types and a coarse grid, and the galleries of false blobs show the worst cases selected by the methods' own responses.

\paragraph{Shift.} The shift study covers a single kind of shift: digits to other digits and a phantom, and CT slices to 20 random piecewise-constant phantoms, which favour TV; no change of scanner geometry, dose model or anatomy was tested. The $K=60$ shift analysis used four lesion types and the other conditions two, and SSIM on the phantoms at $K=60$ was not stored. The error-decomposition statistics cannot separate prior-driven structure from amplified noise except in comparisons with identical noise and settings.
